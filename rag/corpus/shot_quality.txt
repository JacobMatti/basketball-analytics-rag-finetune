Expected Points and Shot Quality

Expected points per shot attempt is a way of scoring how good a shot opportunity was,
independent of whether it actually went in. It is typically modeled using factors like shot
distance, defender proximity at the moment of release, shot clock time remaining, and
whether the shot came off the dribble or a catch. A wide-open three-point shot with a
defender more than six feet away has a much higher expected value than a heavily
contested mid-range pull-up, even if the contested shot happens to fall. Teams use
expected shot value to evaluate offensive process separate from outcome, since a
player or lineup can generate good expected value on a night when shots simply do not
fall, and vice versa. Over a large enough sample, actual points scored tends to
converge toward expected points, which is why decision-makers weight process over
short-term results when evaluating offensive strategy.
