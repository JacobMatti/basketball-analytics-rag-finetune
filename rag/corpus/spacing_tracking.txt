Player Tracking and Spacing Metrics

High-resolution player tracking data captures the x-y position of every player on the
court, typically sampled 25 times per second, along with the ball. From this raw
positional stream, analysts derive spacing metrics such as average distance between
offensive players, the size of driving lanes, and how much floor area a shooter's gravity
opens up for teammates. A common technique is to measure the convex hull area
formed by the five offensive players at the moment a shot is taken; larger hull area
generally correlates with better spacing and higher quality looks for teammates.
Tracking data can also isolate a specific player's gravitational effect by comparing
defender positioning when that player is on versus off the court, holding lineup
otherwise constant. This kind of analysis requires clean, synchronized play-by-play and
tracking data, since spacing metrics are only meaningful when tied to the exact moment
of a specific offensive action.
