Player Load Monitoring and Movement Data

Sports science staffs use tracking-derived movement data, including total distance
covered, number of high-speed accelerations and decelerations, and jump count, to
estimate a player's physical load across a game or practice. Cumulative load over a
rolling multi-game window is compared against a player's established baseline to flag
periods of unusually high workload, which have been associated with elevated
soft-tissue injury risk in sports science research. Because this data is tracking-derived
rather than self-reported, it can be combined with wearable-based metrics like heart
rate variability to build a more complete picture of a player's readiness on a given day.
Teams typically treat any single day's load reading as noisy and instead look at trends
over multiple games, since day-to-day variation in movement data is expected and does
not by itself indicate elevated risk.
